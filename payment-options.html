Let's get you current.

If you're behind on rent, you're not the first and you're not in trouble for reading this. Here's every option we have — pick one and we'll set it up today.

First, the honest part. Nobody here is trying to catch you out. Falling behind usually isn't about not caring — it's about a $775 bill landing on someone who gets paid every Friday. We can fix the timing. We can't fix silence, so talk to us.

Your options

Works for most people

Weekly payments instead of monthly

Same rent, different rhythm. Instead of finding $775 on the 1st, you pay about $179 every Friday — the day you actually get paid.

  • The ask never exceeds one paycheck
  • Nothing to save up for or protect from yourself
  • Set it once and stop thinking about rent
Strongest option if your job allows it

Split your paycheck at work

Most payroll systems let you send part of your check to a second place. Rent comes out before it ever hits your pocket.

  • Same idea as a retirement contribution — you never touch it, so you never have to decide
  • Takes about ten minutes with your employer
  • We'll help you fill out the form
If you're behind already

A written payment plan

We take what you owe, split it into weekly amounts you can actually make, and put it in writing so both sides know exactly what "current" means.

  • You know the number and the finish date
  • Nobody guesses, nobody assumes
  • Being on a plan and paying it counts as current
Any amount, any day

Pay what you can, when you can

$50 counts. $100 counts. Partial payments are applied to your oldest balance first and they are never treated as failure.

  • No minimum
  • Every payment gets a receipt you can keep
Can't pay at all right now

Tell us before it's a problem

Log a pledge — "I can pay $200 on Friday." That's not a lie, that's a plan. What hurts you is going quiet, not being short.

  • Scholarship funds may still be available to you
  • If your funding source ended, we'll look for the next one
  • Work-trade may be an option — ask

Why bother catching up

Not the speech. The actual reasons:

  • A payment history is evidence. Printable proof for a PO, a judge, a landlord, or your family that you do what you say.
  • It's how you leave. Consistent on-time rent is what eventually gets you your own place with your name on the lease.
  • Owing money follows you. Leaving with a balance makes the next house harder to get into. Leaving current makes you a reference.
  • It's one less thing. You've got enough riding on you. Handle this one and it stops sitting on your chest.
One thing that will protect you more than anything on this page: a cushion. $25 a week, untouched, is about $650 in six months. That's the difference between a flat tire being a bad day and a flat tire being the end of everything you built. Ask us about it.
